## Key findings (from text):
- Thermogenic field extends to δ¹³C = −75‰ (early mature) and to −67‰ (low maturity / biodegraded)
- Traditional cutoff of δ¹³C = −55‰ between microbial and thermogenic is inadequate
- 14% of conventional gas samples have δ¹³C < −55‰
- Abiotic gas can have δ¹³C as low as −50‰ (Driefontein, South Africa) or as high as +10‰ (Oman, Greece)
- Primary microbial gas with δ¹³C as high as −32.6‰ reported in endoevaporitic settings (rare)
- Significant overlap between genetic fields — multiple diagrams needed for interpretation
- Oxidation can shift δ¹³C as high as +45‰ and δ²H as high as +301‰
- West Siberia Basin microbial gas (mean δ¹³C = −51.8‰) accounts for ~17% of global conventional gas

## Discussion points:
- Many samples in δ¹³C −60 to −40‰ and δ²H −250 to −150‰ have MIXED origin
- Early mature thermogenic gas can be misidentified as primary microbial
- Biodegradation + secondary methanogenesis creates complex isotopic signatures
